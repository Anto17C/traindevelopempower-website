Course Overview

The complete journey: the AI Practised (Fluency tier) and AI Applied (Mastery tier) courses delivered as one sequenced curriculum, joined by a working bridge rather than a page break. Day 1 builds genuine working capability and closes with each participant nominating one real task from their own role to attempt with AI before Day 2. Day 2 opens on what happened, then builds the full tool stack. Day 3 is given to practical finance applications: case studies, coached exercises and the rebuild of that same task. The arc is simple: design → test → rebuild → embed.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of the programme, participants will be able to:

  • Place their own AI capability on the literacy → fluency → mastery → leadership ladder, at the start and at the close, and name their next step.
  • Run the practice loop (brief, prompt, review, verify, improve, apply) on finance tasks, and check the output rather than relying on the tool.
  • Choose between the leading general-purpose AI models for each task, and use both research modes: closed-document synthesis and live web research.
  • Carry a finance document through to a finished presentation, and use voice input across the tool stack.
  • Apply the compliance boundary (CBUAE, SCA) across single tools, multi-tool workflows, voice and AI agents.
  • Design, test and rebuild a personal AI workflow for a task from their own role, with a measured before and after: evidence of improvement, not just exposure to tools.

Course Outline

18 Modules over 3 Days

Day 1: AI Practised: from awareness to working capability

1 Opening: Why AI, Why You, Why Now

The commercial impact of AI on regulated finance today. The literacy → fluency → mastery → leadership ladder, and what each tier looks like in practice.

  • Activity: Ladder self-assessment, re-marked at the close of Day 3

2 Mindset and Principles

AI as a thinking partner, not a search engine. The practice loop behind all AI work: brief, prompt, review, verify, improve, apply. Verification, and the data and compliance boundary (CBUAE, SCA).

  • Discussion: What separates productive AI use from fumbling

3 Live Demonstrations: How AI Actually Works

3 demonstrations on real finance material: closed-document research, a prompt-to-output workflow on a finance task, and a voice-driven, multi-tool workflow. Plus a short preview of AI that does the work, not just advises. Participant output, not trainer performance, is the day's measure.

  • Case Study: Closed-document research on a real regulatory or product document, supplied by the trainer

4 Prompting Workshop (hands-on)

The first of the day's 2 centrepieces. Participants work the practice loop through a task they pick from their own role, such as a client suitability narrative, a regulatory summary or a portfolio review note, using trainer-supplied sample material, so no confidential data is used.

  • Activity: Coached prompting, with outputs critiqued live for finance accuracy and compliance fit

5 The Tool Stack in Practice

When to use which model and why. Side-by-side comparisons on the same finance task. Voice-driven prompting practised, not just demonstrated. Each participant starts a working personal tool stack.

  • Activity: Side-by-side comparison of leading assistants on one finance task

6 The Compliance Boundary

What stays out of public AI tools, what may safely go in, and where the grey zones sit, anchored in CBUAE and SCA expectations. The boundary map built here becomes a live constraint on Day 3.

  • Case Study: 3 to 4 trainer-supplied scenarios from wealth, compliance, risk and treasury work

7 Group Challenge: Build a Finance Workflow

The day's second centrepiece. Teams of 3 to 4 tackle a realistic finance brief using any combination of tools from the day. Teams produce, present and debrief; the trainer critiques both the output and the workflow.

  • Activity: Team build, presentation and debrief

8 Close of Day 1: First Stack and the Bridge Task

Each participant documents a first personal AI stack and nominates one task from their own role to attempt with AI before Day 2, with no client or confidential data. No polish expected: the fumbles are the curriculum.

  • Activity: The bridge task: one real task, attempted alone

Day 2: AI Applied: the full tool stack, one capability at a time

9 The Bridge Review

What happened. Successes are dissected for what made them work; fumbles are diagnosed live: wrong tool, prompt or task, or the wrong side of the compliance boundary.

  • Discussion: Bridge tasks diagnosed live; what the room brings in sets the day's emphasis

10 Comparing the General-Purpose Models

From using AI tools one at a time to designing how they work together. Which model for which task: the leading assistants side by side on the same finance tasks.

  • Activity: Apply a decision framework for when to use which model, and why

11 Research and Knowledge: Two Modes

Closed-document research and synthesis versus live web research, and when each mode is the right one.

  • Case Study: Hands-on with a fund prospectus, a regulatory circular or a research note

12 Creating and Presenting with AI

The AI presentation landscape compared: content generation, design, and direct connectors that collapse the document-to-deck workflow into a single step. Includes an honest cost and access guide.

  • Activity: Carry a finance document through to a finished presentation

13 Voice Input as a Productivity Layer

Native dictation where it is strong, and a cross-application voice layer for the rest, with finance use cases.

  • Activity: Hands-on voice setup and practice

Day 3: Applied in finance: case studies, coached builds and the capstones

14 Designing Your Personal Stack

Chaining tools into workflows that multiply output without multiplying effort, anchored in one discipline: task → tool choice → workflow chain → verification → controlled output.

  • Case Study: 4 live finance pipeline demonstrations
  • Activity: Map a personal stack against your own role

15 The Agentic Frontier

Desktop-level AI carrying out a real multi-step finance task, shown live. Framed for regulated firms: where the data boundary sits, and why human approval and auditability decide what can be deployed.

  • Discussion: Which workflows should a regulated firm pilot now, and which should it watch?

16 Capstone 1: Rebuild the Bridge Task

Each participant rebuilds their Day 1 bridge task with the full stack: the same task, attempted twice, and a complete workflow executed, not just planned. The before and after is the proof of progress.

  • Activity: Coached individual rebuild: the trainer circulates and coaches

17 Capstone 2: Team Finance Challenge

Teams of 3 to 4 work a realistic brief, such as a market-event note for a client segment, ending in a client-ready presentation pack. At least 3 tools, the Day 1 boundary map applied throughout, and a real deadline.

  • Case Study: A trainer-supplied finance brief
  • Activity: Teams present; the debrief critiques the output, the workflow and the verification discipline

18 Close: Movement on the Ladder

The self-assessment from Day 1 is re-run. Each participant marks their movement, documents their rebuilt personal stack, and leaves with 2 concrete actions for the month ahead.

  • Activity: Self-assessment re-marked
Participant Takeaways
  • A personal AI workflow, designed, tested and rebuilt, with a measured before and after.
  • A documented personal AI stack and 2 concrete actions for the month ahead.
  • A compliance boundary map, taught in cases and applied in practice.
  • A team-tested finance deliverable.
  • Their before-and-after position on the AI ladder.
Delivery Format and Preparation

Format

  • Hands-on throughout, with all case studies and sample material supplied by the trainer.
  • Best with up to 15 participants, so everyone gets coached, not just shown.
  • Tools are shown as examples of current practice, not as product endorsements.

What to Bring

  • A laptop with internet access.
  • No technical background needed.
  • No client or firm data: own-role tasks are described in general terms.
  • A joining note before the course lists the tool accounts to set up.

Software access and any paid subscription requirements are still being confirmed with the trainer. We will list the tool accounts to set up in your joining note.
