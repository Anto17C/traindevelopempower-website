How to Check AI-Generated Financial Analysis

A worked, fictional example of checking AI-written financial commentary against the source figures.

Revenue can rise while operating profit falls. An AI-written commentary that misses that distinction can give a manager the wrong picture of performance. Before using a generated financial summary, check the source figures, reproduce the calculations and test whether each conclusion follows from the evidence.

Generative AI can produce plausible statements that are inaccurate or unsupported. NIST identifies this risk in its Generative AI Profile. A polished explanation therefore needs the same scrutiny as the figures it describes. NIST Generative AI Profile

Define the task and the permitted information

Start with a specific output. For example, ask for a short commentary comparing revenue, gross margin and operating profit across two periods, with a calculation supporting each statement. Specify the currency, reporting period, units and intended reader.

Use an AI environment and information that your organisation has approved for the task. Check what information may be entered and how the output may be used. The example below uses entirely fictional figures so the exercise can be repeated without client or confidential company data.

For document-based work, identify the report, version and relevant pages or tables. For external research, record the source and its publication date. A source reference should make it possible for another reviewer to find the underlying evidence.

Work through a fictional performance example

The following simplified management figures describe two comparable periods. All amounts are in US dollars. Direct costs and operating overheads are shown as positive expenses.

Fictional management figures for two periods (US$, except margin)

Revenue1,000,0001,200,000
Direct costs600,000780,000
Gross profit400,000420,000
Gross margin40%35%

Operating profit150,000120,000

For this example, gross profit equals revenue less direct costs. Gross margin equals gross profit divided by revenue. Operating profit equals gross profit less operating overheads.

A useful instruction for an AI assistant would be the following.

Using only the supplied figures, draft a short performance commentary for a finance manager. Compare revenue, gross profit, gross margin and operating profit. Show the arithmetic supporting each change. Distinguish percentage changes from percentage-point movements. Identify questions requiring further evidence and keep explanations of causes separate from the observed figures.

An illustrative flawed commentary might state that revenue grew by 20%, gross profit increased by 20% and profitability improved. This commentary is invented for the exercise.

The revenue calculation is correct. The other statements need correction.

Recalculate the changes

Check the arithmetic in a spreadsheet or another calculation tool, using the source figures. Keep the formulas available for review.

Recalculating the fictional figures
CheckCalculationResult
Revenue growth(1,200,000 − 1,000,000) ÷ 1,000,00020%
Gross profit growth(420,000 − 400,000) ÷ 400,0005%
Gross margin movement35% − 40%−5 percentage points
Operating profit change(120,000 − 150,000) ÷ 150,000−20%

Gross profit increased by US$20,000, while gross margin declined. A five-percentage-point decline from 40% to 35% represents a 12.5% relative reduction in the margin. Use the measure that matches the statement being made and label it clearly.

You can also reconcile the operating-profit movement. Revenue increased by US$200,000, direct costs increased by US$180,000 and overheads increased by US$50,000. Those movements produce a US$30,000 reduction in operating profit.

This reconciliation gives a reviewer another way to test the commentary. A draft describing improved operating profitability would conflict with the figures.

Check the explanation against the evidence

The table supports a statement that direct costs grew faster than revenue. It does not establish whether the reason was supplier pricing, sales mix, discounting, efficiency or a change in how costs were classified.

Ask for the additional information needed to investigate those possibilities. Sales quantities and realised prices could help explain revenue changes. Cost breakdowns, supplier invoices and classification notes could help explain the increase in direct costs. Keep an untested explanation labelled as a question until supporting evidence is available.

Apply the same discipline when an AI-generated report cites an external source. Open the reference, find the relevant passage and check its date and scope. The House of Commons Library recommends checking generated factual claims against authoritative evidence and reviewing the output with subject expertise. House of Commons Library guidance on checking AI-generated information

Revise the commentary and keep a review record

A corrected commentary for the fictional figures could read as follows.

Revenue increased by 20% to US$1.2 million. Gross profit rose by 5% to US$420,000, while gross margin fell from 40% to 35%. Direct costs increased by 30%, outpacing revenue growth. Operating overheads rose by 20%, and operating profit fell by 20% to US$120,000. Further analysis of pricing, sales mix and cost movements is needed to explain the margin decline.

Keep the source table, calculations, revised commentary and reviewer approval together. If the figures change, repeat the checks before circulating the commentary. For recurring reporting, include the source version and review date so the next person can see which information was used.

The same evidence trail helps directors judge a proposal to use AI for reporting. Our companion article, What Boards Should Ask Before Approving an AI Initiative, explains how checks like these fit an approval decision.

Apply the process to everyday finance work

The same checks can support a portfolio-review note, a product-document summary, a regulatory briefing or a management presentation. Define the task, identify permitted sources, check calculations and factual claims, then review the interpretation before using the result.
